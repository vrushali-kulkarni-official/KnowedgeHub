Act 1 · Step 1.3

The Document Skeleton

Know what belongs in <head> vs <body>, and why the three meta tags matter.

What to notice

  • <title> inside <head> is the browser tab text — and also what shows up in search results and bookmarks.
  • defer on a <script> means: download it in parallel, but run it after the HTML is parsed. You'll learn why that matters in Act 7.

Look at this page's own head

Right-click anywhere → View Page Source for the raw source, versus Inspect for the parsed DOM. In DevTools, the <head> node shows the browser's version, which includes elements you never wrote (<meta name="viewport"> may have been auto-added, and <base> sometimes appears).

Challenge — Three skeletons, one difference

  1. Write a page with a <title> of "My Portfolio — Ana Silva" and a lang of pt-BR. Confirm the tab title changed.
  2. Delete the viewport meta and note the difference in how the page lays out (narrow the browser window to simulate a phone). Put it back.
  3. Add a <meta name="description" content="..."> with a one-sentence description. This is what search engines and social cards show — you'll see it again in Act 11.
  4. Put a <script src="does-not-exist.js"> in the head. Watch the Network panel. What happens, and why doesn't the page break?
Stuck? Open the hints
  • A 404 in the Network panel is not a page error — the browser just moves on. That's the same forgiving-parser philosophy as before, applied to resources.
  • Missing viewport makes mobile browsers scale the page down, so everything looks tiny and unreadable.
